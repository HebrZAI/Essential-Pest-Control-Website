A healthier, more manageable yard

Pests Affect More Than the Appearance of a Stable

Persistent pest activity can unsettle horses, contaminate feed and create avoidable work for grooms, instructors and yard managers.

Horse comfort and wellbeing

Heavy fly and mosquito pressure can cause constant irritation, interfere with rest and make horses difficult to handle in stalls and work areas.

Feed contamination and waste

Rodents and stored-product insects can foul or damage feed. Affected bags may need to be discarded, increasing costs and disrupting feeding routines.

Reputation and client confidence

Owners boarding horses expect a well-run facility. Visible rats, excessive flies or contaminated storage areas quickly undermine confidence in the yard.

Look beyond the individual stall

High-Risk Areas Around an Equestrian Property

01

Stalls & stable passages

Spilt feed, damp bedding, water troughs and sheltered edges can support flies, ants, cockroaches and rodents close to horses.

02

Feed rooms & storage

Open bags, loose grain and clutter attract rats, mice and stored-product insects. Rodent-proof storage and early detection are essential.

03

Manure and bedding areas

Manure piles, used bedding and slow removal cycles create major fly-breeding pressure, especially during warm or wet Gauteng weather.

04

Tack rooms & offices

Quiet rooms provide shelter for rodents and crawling insects, with the potential for damage to stored equipment, wiring and personal items.

05

Wash bays, drains & troughs

Standing water, leaking fittings and organic build-up can support flies, mosquitoes and cockroaches around heavily used areas.

06

Perimeters & paddock edges

Long grass, vegetation, neighbouring land, refuse and damaged structures influence rodent, tick and other pest pressure around the yard.

Questions from stable owners and yard managers

Horse Stable & Equestrian Pest Control FAQs

Is pest control safe around horses?

It can be carried out responsibly when the treatment is selected and planned for the actual site. We consider horse access, exposed feed and water, ventilation and stable routines, and provide preparation or re-entry instructions where needed.

Will the horses need to leave the property?

Usually not. They may need to be moved away from a specific treatment area for a stated period, depending on the pest, product and location. We confirm this before work begins.

How can we reduce flies around the stables?

Effective fly control usually combines targeted treatment with frequent manure removal, dry bedding, clean drains, repaired leaks and attention to other breeding material. Treatment alone cannot compensate for persistent breeding conditions.

How often should an equestrian facility receive pest control?

That depends on horse numbers, manure handling, feed storage, the surrounding environment and pest history. Some yards need seasonal help; higher-risk facilities benefit from scheduled monitoring.

Can rodent bait be used around horses and stable pets?

Rodent control must be carefully assessed. Where bait is suitable, it should be secured in tamper-resistant stations positioned to limit access by horses, pets, wildlife and unauthorised people.

Do you treat feed rooms and tack rooms?

Yes, after inspecting the area and identifying the pest. Exposed feed, supplements, equipment and water may need to be removed or protected before a targeted treatment is completed.
