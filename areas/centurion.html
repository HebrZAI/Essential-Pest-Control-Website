A local, property-first approach

Complexes and estates change how pests move

In Centurion, the place where a pest appears is not always where the problem starts. Cockroaches can travel along shared service routes, rodents may cross boundary walls and roof lines, and ants can nest outdoors before entering several kitchens.

A useful inspection therefore considers the affected unit together with drains, refuse areas, garages, gardens, roof voids and adjoining spaces where relevant. Treating one room repeatedly without checking those connections can leave the original pressure untouched.

Centurion questions

Planning a Centurion residential or complex visit

Should adjoining units be inspected for cockroaches?

It depends on the building and evidence. If activity appears linked to shared walls, plumbing, ducts or refuse areas, checking adjoining units and common spaces can prevent an incomplete one-unit response.

Can trustees arrange one coordinated treatment day?

Yes. Provide the number of units, common areas, reported pests and access constraints. We can then discuss a workable sequence and preparation information for occupants.

When should termite treatment be booked during construction?

Speak to the pest-control provider before the relevant soil or building stage is covered. Timing depends on the construction sequence, site preparation and selected treatment specification.

Do you service Centurion business parks?

Yes, subject to route availability and site requirements. Tell us the business type, operating hours, affected areas and whether you need a once-off response or ongoing monitoring.
