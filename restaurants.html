Why this matters more in your industry

In food service, a pest problem is never just a pest problem.

Your margins are tight, your health department visits are unannounced, and a single customer photo can travel further than any marketing campaign you've paid for. Here's what's actually at stake.

Regulatory shutdown risk

An environmental health inspector who finds evidence of infestation can issue notices that halt trade on the spot - lost revenue for every hour you're closed, plus the cost of remedial action to reopen.

Reviews you can't take back

One photo or video of a cockroach or rodent near a dining area tends to outlast any response you post. It gets screenshotted, shared, and remembered long after the issue is resolved.

Stock and supply losses

Rodents and stored-product pests contaminate dry goods, packaging and ingredients - meaning wasted stock, disrupted prep, and menu items pulled at short notice.

What we're actually watching for

The Pests That Target Commercial Kitchens

01

German cockroaches

Warm equipment, grease traps and tight cracks near cooking lines give them everything they need - and they spread fast between shared walls in strip malls and food courts.

02

Rodents (rats & mice)

Drawn to waste areas, delivery docks and dry storage. Beyond the health risk, gnaw damage to wiring and packaging is a fire and stock-loss risk most owners don't budget for.

03

Fruit flies & drain flies

Breeding in drains, waste bins and overripe produce. A visible cloud near a bar or prep station is one of the fastest ways to lose customer confidence at the table.

04

Stored product pests

Weevils, beetles and moths work their way into flour, grain, spice and packaged dry goods - often arriving in with a delivery, unnoticed until stock is already compromised.

05

Ants

Sugar spills, syrup stations and open packaging draw foraging trails that lead straight back to prep surfaces and customer-facing areas.

06

Birds & pigeons (outdoor seating)

Nesting near extraction units and outdoor dining areas brings droppings and debris that health inspectors flag on sight - and customers notice even faster.

Questions we hear from operators

Restaurant & Fast Food Pest Control FAQs

Can you work after hours so it doesn't affect service?

Yes - this is the default for most of our restaurant clients. We schedule around your trading hours, typically before opening or after close, so treatment never overlaps with prep or service.

Will I have something to show a health inspector?

Yes. Every visit ends with a certificate of service and a written record, kept as part of an ongoing compliance folder - so you're never caught without documentation.

Do you work with multi-site franchise groups?

Yes, we set up consistent programs across multiple locations with centralised reporting, so head office and individual site managers both have visibility.

Are the products safe to use around food prep areas?

Yes - placement and product selection are chosen specifically to keep treatment away from food-contact surfaces, with methods suited to an operating commercial kitchen.

What happens if we spot something between scheduled visits?

Contact us and we'll prioritise a same-week callout. Active sightings in a food environment aren't something we let sit until the next routine visit.

Do you service takeaways and cloud kitchens, not just sit-down restaurants?

Yes - the same risk-based approach applies whether you're running a full dining room, a quick-service counter, or a delivery-only kitchen with no public seating area at all.
